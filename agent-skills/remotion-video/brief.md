# Video brief

This brief guides the agent that makes the video. Treat the job as filmmaking first and coding second. Judge every choice by what the viewer sees, reads and hears, and when. Framing, type, timing and story count as much as clean code.

## The film

- **Purpose:** [promo, launch, explainer, demo or social clip]
- **Product:** [name and web address]
- **What it is:** [one or two plain sentences]
- **Who watches:** [the people it's for]
- **What they should take away:** [the one benefit that matters most]
- **Lines approved word for word:** [exact lines, or "none"]
- **Last frame:** [for example the logo, a short line and the address]
- **Length and format:** [about N seconds; default 16:9, 1920×1080, 30 fps]. Keep all layout numbers in one place, so another format is a small job later.
- **Where it will be posted:** [site, social, video platforms]

## Source material

- **Read from:** [folders and links]. Treat these as read-only. Copy what you need into the video project and never change the originals.
- **Off limits:** [paid or private material that must not be shown or quoted]
- **Logo and images:** [files]
- **Fonts:** [files, and what each one is used for]. Copy the files in; don't fetch lookalikes.
- **Colours:** [each colour and its job: background, text, accent, depth]. Leave out: [colours reserved for something else, and why]

## Words on screen

- Every line comes from the owner's own material. Cutting words is fine. Show any new wording to the owner before using it.
- **Tone:** [for example plain and descriptive, or light and playful]. Never hype or a sales voice.
- No prices, discounts, user counts, ratings, testimonials or performance claims, unless the owner supplies them for this video.
- The video must be readable on a phone. At 1080p, keep headlines at about 72 px or more and everything else at about 36 px or more.

## Look and motion

- **Mood:** [a few words, for example calm and technical]
- Use the accent colour sparingly, so that it always points at something.
- Movement is short and eased, with a few deliberate camera moves and then stillness. Nothing moves all the time, spins or flashes. Each picture comes to rest before the next one starts.
- Vary the framing: show the whole thing, then move in on the one detail that matters and mark it lightly.

## How it's built

- One component per scene, plus shared parts that scenes reuse.
- Copy, colours, fonts, asset paths and timings live in one config file, apart from the animation code.
- First get the whole film playing end to end, then polish single moments.

## Before it's done

Watch it through, and check stills from every scene:

- The first second is understood without effort.
- Each scene gives the eye one place to land.
- Every line stays up long enough to read.
- Images are sharp and keep their proportions.
- Nothing is cut off, and nothing overlaps.
- No line of text ends on a single word.
- The ending feels chosen, and the last frame stays put long enough to take in.

If a scene feels hurried, bare or busy, rework it.

## Hand-over

- The storyboard, approved before animation.
- The project.
- The video as an MP4: H.264, at the size and frame rate above.
- A short note on changing the copy, images, colours and timing.
- Any assumptions, stated briefly.
